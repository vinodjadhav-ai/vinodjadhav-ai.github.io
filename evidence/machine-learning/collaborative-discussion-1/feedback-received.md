# Collaborative Discussion 1 — Feedback Received

**Topic:** Industry 4.0 / Industry 5.0, telecommunications resilience and the 2022 Rogers outage  
**Evidence:** Two classmates responded to my initial forum post in August 2026. The original replies are preserved in the [forum feedback screenshot](screenshots/Discussion-1_Peer_feedback.png).

## Feedback from classmate 1

The reply agreed that the Rogers outage showed why critical network infrastructure cannot be assessed by efficiency and availability alone. It drew attention to the configuration change that removed a routing policy filter, the wider failure of change-management safeguards and the importance of tested rollback plans. The classmate also suggested separating management connectivity and maintaining independent connectivity for critical emergency services such as 9-1-1.

## Feedback from classmate 2

The second reply explored preventive measures more closely, including mandatory peer review of changes, testing in a staging environment, incremental deployment with automatic rollback, and independent out-of-band management. It also raised deliberate failure testing (chaos engineering) as a way to check whether systems degrade gracefully, asking whether controls for critical infrastructure should be voluntary or mandatory.

## How the feedback influenced my learning

My original post placed considerable emphasis on network telemetry and anomaly detection. These replies helped me recognise that better detection is not sufficient on its own. Configuration validation, independent management access, staged changes, rollback and regular recovery exercises matter just as much. I incorporated these ideas into my final summary post, with greater emphasis on human judgement and resilience designed into the network.
