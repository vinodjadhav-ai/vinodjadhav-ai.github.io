# Collaborative Discussion 2 — Short Learning Reflection

## What?

I discussed opportunities and concerns around AI-generated writing, drawing on Hutson (2021) and my experience writing technical network reports. I posted an initial contribution, replied to two classmates and prepared a summary post. I also received a response suggesting practical controls for incident reporting.

## So What?

Initially, I focused mainly on the risk of confident but inaccurate information. The peer discussion helped me consider a more balanced approach. Low-risk tasks such as draft emails can be handled differently from incident reports, medical guidance or legal documents. I also learned that rejecting AI because it makes occasional mistakes is not sufficient: error severity, human comparisons and safeguards matter. Feedback about relying on source records and including verification time in productivity calculations was particularly useful.

## Now What?

In future, I would treat an AI-generated technical report as a draft only. I would check its claims against approved incident logs and investigation records, distinguish confirmed from uncertain causes, protect confidential information and require an accountable human reviewer. I would evaluate whether the tool saves total time after these checks, not just whether it writes quickly.
