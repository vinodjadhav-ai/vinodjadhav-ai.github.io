# MSc Artificial Intelligence e-Portfolio Writing Guidelines

**Author:** Vinod Jadhav  
**Programme:** MSc Artificial Intelligence - University of Essex

---

# Status

Active

This document defines the writing style that will be used throughout my MSc Artificial Intelligence e-Portfolio.

It is intended to ensure consistency, authenticity, and alignment with the University's expectations for reflective academic writing.

---

# Purpose

The purpose of these guidelines is to help me write consistently across all modules.

Rather than trying to sound highly academic or overly technical, I want my portfolio to accurately represent my own learning journey throughout the MSc programme.

The writing should demonstrate gradual development in knowledge, practical experience, and professional confidence.

---

# My Writing Voice

I will write as:

- someone who has been studying AI for approximately 7–8 months;
- an experienced enterprise networking professional transitioning into AI;
- a learner who is building confidence through practical work and reflection.

The portfolio should demonstrate curiosity, continuous learning, and honest self-evaluation.

---

# General Writing Principles

## Write in the First Person

Examples:

"I learned..."

"I realised..."

"I found..."

"I initially thought..."

"My understanding changed..."

This keeps reflections personal and authentic.

---

## Be Honest

It is acceptable to discuss:

- mistakes;
- uncertainty;
- challenges;
- concepts that were initially difficult;
- areas requiring further development.

Reflection is about learning, not perfection.

---

## Explain Rather Than Impress

Avoid unnecessarily complicated language.

If a concept can be explained clearly using simple English, choose the simpler explanation.

The objective is effective communication rather than sounding overly academic.

---

## Show Learning

Every major section should answer questions such as:

- What did I learn?

- Why was it important?

- How did my understanding change?

- How will I use this knowledge in future?

---

## Support Reflection with Evidence

Where appropriate, refer to:

- discussion activities;
- practical exercises;
- coding activities;
- project work;
- testing;
- screenshots;
- peer feedback;
- tutor feedback.

Reflection should be supported by evidence whenever possible.

---

# Writing Style

I will aim for writing that is:

- clear;
- structured;
- reflective;
- evidence-based;
- professional;
- concise.

---

# Tone

The tone should remain:

Professional

Honest

Reflective

Positive

Balanced

Avoid:

Overconfidence

Exaggeration

Overly emotional language

Claims that cannot be supported

---

# Academic Style

The portfolio is not intended to read like a research paper.

Instead, it should combine:

- academic understanding;
- professional experience;
- practical evidence;
- personal reflection.

Whenever literature is referenced, appropriate Harvard referencing should be used.

---

# Reflection Style

Most reflections should naturally follow three stages.

## What?

Briefly describe the activity.

---

## So What?

Explain why it mattered.

Discuss what was learned.

Identify any challenges or changes in understanding.

---

## Now What?

Explain how this experience will influence future learning or professional practice.

---

# Connecting My Professional Background

Where appropriate, I will connect new Artificial Intelligence concepts with my previous experience in enterprise networking.

However, I will avoid forcing comparisons where no meaningful connection exists.

The emphasis should remain on demonstrating genuine learning rather than attempting to present myself as an expert in AI.

---

# Common Mistakes to Avoid

Do not simply describe activities.

Do not copy module notes.

Do not repeat assessment guidance.

Do not claim complete mastery of topics that I am still learning.

Do not include unnecessary technical detail if it does not support reflection.

Do not write paragraphs that add no value to the assessment.

---

# Before Finalising Any Section

Before adding content to the e-Portfolio, I will ask:


- Does it demonstrate learning?

- Does it include evidence?

- Does it support a learning outcome?

- Does it contribute towards the marking criteria?

- Would I naturally explain this in the same way during a conversation?

If the answer to any question is "No", I should revise the section.

---

# How I Will Use This Document

I will review these guidelines before writing any new section of my e-Portfolio.

The objective is not to produce perfect academic writing.

The objective is to produce an honest, reflective, and evidence-based record of my development throughout the MSc Artificial Intelligence programme.
