# Purpose

Assessment briefs are often written using formal academic language. As a student, I found that it is sometimes difficult to understand exactly what is expected.

The purpose of this document is to translate assessment requirements into simple, practical actions that I can follow while developing my e-Portfolio.

Rather than asking:

> "What should I write?"

I want to ask:

> "What evidence do I need to demonstrate?"

This approach helps ensure that every section of my e-Portfolio has a clear purpose and contributes towards the learning outcomes and marking criteria.

---

# My Approach

For every module I will follow the same process.

## Step 1

Read the assessment brief carefully.

---

## Step 2

Identify:

- learning outcomes
- e-Portfolio activities
- assessment requirements
- marking criteria
- required evidence
- word limits

---

## Step 3

Translate each requirement into plain English.

---

## Step 4

Identify what evidence I already have.

---

## Step 5

Identify any missing evidence.

---

## Step 6

Write reflections that explain:

- what I learned
- why it mattered
- how my understanding changed
- how I will apply the learning in future

# Requirement Translation Framework

The following table helps me convert academic requirements into practical actions.

| Assessment Requirement | What it Means in Practice | Evidence to Include |
|-------------------------|---------------------------|---------------------|
| Summary of Learning Outcomes | Explain the purpose of the module in my own words. | Module overview and learning outcomes page. |
| Collaborative Discussions | Demonstrate how my understanding developed through discussions with classmates. | Initial post, peer responses, summary post, reflection. |
| Team Exercises | Show my own contribution to group activities and explain what I learned. | Team proposal, meeting notes, design decisions, contribution summary. |
| Practical Activities | Include outputs created during the module. | Coding exercises, KQML dialogue, Parse Tree, Deep Learning activity, screenshots. |
| Individual Project | Explain how I designed, implemented, tested and evaluated my own solution. | Inquiro project, testing, README, presentation, GitHub repository. |
| Reflection | Explain what changed in my thinking throughout the module. | Final reflection using What – So What – Now What. |
| Professional Skills | Demonstrate skills developed during the module. | Skills Matrix and PDP. |
| Evidence | Support reflections with examples wherever possible. | Screenshots, GitHub commits, reports, discussions, project artefacts. |

# Learning Outcome Mapping

Every major section should support one or more learning outcomes.

Instead of writing first and mapping later, I will identify the learning outcomes before writing.

This helps ensure that every section contributes towards the assessment requirements.

---

# Marking Criteria Mapping

Before completing any section I will ask:

## Criticality

Have I explained how my thinking changed?

---

## Application of Knowledge

Have I shown how I applied theory to practical work?

---

## Collaboration

Have I demonstrated my own contribution?

---

## Academic Integrity

Have I referenced sources appropriately?

---

## Structure and Presentation

Is the section organised and easy to follow?

---

# Reflection Checklist

Before considering any section complete, I will ask:

- What did I do?
- Why did I do it?
- What difficulties did I experience?
- What did I learn?
- What would I improve next time?
- How does this relate to the learning outcomes?
- What evidence supports my reflection?

# My Personal Reminder

The purpose of the e-Portfolio is not to prove that I know everything.

The purpose is to demonstrate how I have developed throughout the module.

A genuine learning journey that includes challenges, improvements, and reflection is more valuable than trying to present every activity as a perfect success.
