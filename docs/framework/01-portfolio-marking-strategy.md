# Purpose

The purpose of this document is to translate the University's marking criteria into a practical working strategy.

Rather than writing content first and hoping it meets the assessment requirements, I will use this document to ensure that every piece of evidence, reflection, and project artefact has a clear purpose and contributes towards the final mark.

This document will be reviewed whenever I prepare content for any module.

---

# Assessment Philosophy

The University's assessment is not based on the appearance of the website.

Instead, the emphasis is placed on:

- evidence of learning;
- critical reflection;
- application of knowledge;
- collaboration;
- academic integrity;
- professional development.

My objective is therefore to maximise evidence of learning rather than simply increase the amount of written content.

---

# Assessment Criteria

| Assessment Area | Weight | What the Assessor is Looking For |
|-----------------|:------:|----------------------------------|
| Criticality | **40%** | Evidence of critical thinking, reflection, evaluation, and learning from experience. |
| Application of Knowledge | **20%** | Ability to apply theoretical concepts to practical work and real-world situations. |
| Collaboration | **20%** | Evidence of teamwork, discussion participation, and individual contribution. |
| Academic Integrity | **10%** | Appropriate referencing, citations, and honest academic practice. |
| Structure & Presentation | **10%** | Logical organisation, professional presentation, and clear communication. |

---

# How I Will Maximise Marks

## 1. Criticality (40%)

This is the highest-weighted assessment criterion.

Every major section should demonstrate:

- how my understanding changed;
- why I made particular design decisions;
- difficulties encountered during learning;
- limitations of my work;
- improvements I would make in future.

Instead of simply describing what I completed, I will explain what I learned from the experience.

---

## 2. Application of Knowledge (20%)

I will demonstrate that theoretical concepts have been applied to practical work.

Examples include:

- collaborative discussions;
- practical activities;
- coding exercises;
- system design;
- implementation;
- testing;
- evaluation.

Whenever possible, I will connect module concepts with my previous professional experience in enterprise networking while acknowledging that I am still developing my Artificial Intelligence knowledge.

---

## 3. Collaboration (20%)

Collaboration extends beyond team meetings.

Evidence may include:

- discussion forum participation;
- peer responses;
- team design activities;
- project meetings;
- individual contribution to group work;
- feedback received from peers and tutors.

My reflections should explain how collaboration influenced my learning rather than simply recording attendance.

---

## 4. Academic Integrity (10%)

Every section should demonstrate good academic practice.

This includes:

- Harvard referencing;
- accurate citations;
- appropriate acknowledgement of ideas;
- honest reflection;
- accurate representation of my own contribution.

I will avoid making exaggerated claims regarding my knowledge or contribution.

---

## 5. Structure and Presentation (10%)

The website should remain:

- well organised;
- easy to navigate;
- visually consistent;
- professionally presented.

The website design should support the content rather than distract from it.

---

# Evidence Mapping Strategy

Before writing any section, I will identify the supporting evidence.

Possible evidence includes:

- discussion forum posts;
- peer responses;
- summary posts;
- unit activities;
- coding exercises;
- system designs;
- GitHub repositories;
- screenshots;
- testing evidence;
- presentations;
- meeting notes;
- tutor feedback.

Every reflection should reference one or more pieces of evidence wherever appropriate.

---

# Reflection Strategy

Whenever I write a reflection, I will try to answer the following questions.

## What happened?

Describe the activity or experience.

## Why was it important?

Explain what I learned.

## What changed?

Describe how my understanding developed.

## What would I do differently?

Identify improvements for future work.

This approach follows the reflective practice encouraged throughout the MSc programme.

---

# Quality Checklist

Before adding any content to the e-Portfolio, I will ask the following questions.

## Evidence

- Does this section contain supporting evidence?

## Learning Outcomes

- Does it demonstrate one or more module learning outcomes?

## Reflection

- Does it explain what I learned rather than simply describing the activity?

## Critical Thinking

- Have I evaluated my own work?

## Collaboration

- Have I explained my own contribution where appropriate?

## References

- Have I included appropriate citations where required?

## Authenticity

- Does this sound like my own learning journey?

# Personal Reminder

My objective is not to write the longest portfolio.

My objective is to produce a portfolio that demonstrates genuine learning, professional development, and evidence-based reflection.

Every paragraph should contribute towards the assessment criteria.

If a paragraph does not support the assessment requirements, it should be reconsidered before being included.
